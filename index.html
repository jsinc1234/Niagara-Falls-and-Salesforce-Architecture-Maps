Shared foundations: build once, use everywhere

  • One knowledge base serves front-counter staff, the portal, the app, the resident agent and IT self-service, so residents and staff get the same answer everywhere.
  • Reusable APIs (starting with tax and billing) feed the staff console first, then the portal, app and agent. Channels stay protected when back-office systems change.
  • Employee data from Workday can drive onboarding and offboarding tasks in IT service management, and power an employee help agent.
  • Payments taken on the platform still post to the finance system, so payments and integration should be planned together.
  • Better tools for customer service staff also reduce IT requests, since front-line teams touch the most systems.

Decisions ahead for the City

  1. Platform role: a corporate service platform, or a departmental tool?
  2. Integration ownership: an in-house team, a partner, or a mix, sized to the City's capacity.
  3. Sequencing with the finance system: build connections now, or align with the replacement timeline?
  4. Resident identity: one login across portal, app, recreation and payments?
  5. AI guardrails: what data agents can use, in line with MFIPPA, AODA and cybersecurity priorities.
  6. Funding approach: phased investments aligned to annual budget cycles and procurement rules.
